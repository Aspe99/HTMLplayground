Trains

I love trains. My preference for trains lies around 30 years ago, with my favourite Class being the 323. Below I explain a bit about this.

Train?

Most people think that anything that runs on rails is a train, however this is simply not true. A 'train' is only a 'train' once some method of hauling power has been added to some form of transportation, such as carraiges or freight wagons. This is how I see it. Older stock had 'locomotives', which were just the raw hauling power. Modern stock are 'multiple units', but I'll get more into that later.

Classes

Modern trains fit into classes, where you have many of the same build together in one class.. Older trains were 'locomotives', such as the Class 37, the Class 66 and the Class 47 to name a few. What makes these different to the modern day, is that they are raw hauling power, with a drivers seat in them. Many are still used today for the purposes of freight, rescue operations and in some cases, passenger operations.
Trains are sorted by number, for example, 37xyz, where the letters would be other digits. Another example is 730xyz. The letters 'xyz' would obviously be digits. As you might have noticed, there are always three identifying digits. The last three digits represent that unit number, and the remaining preceding numbers represent the class number. To put this into practice, 730024 and 730025 are both class 730s, however the are different Multiple Units.

Multiple Units

I mentioned locomotives before, and how we have to couple carriages to them. A multiple unit is effectively an all in one application. It is made up of cars, each having a different role. Usually, the end cars will have the driving cab. Lets take a Class 323 for example. Its formation is DMSO, PTSO, DMSO. The 'D' represents 'Driving', telling us that there is a driver's cab. The 'M' represents 'Motor', telling us that this car has motors which are moving the train. The 'S' tells us the type of seating, in this case 'Standard Class', and the 'O' tells us the seating configuration, which in this case is 'Open', compared to the closed cabin seating on older steam wagons. The 'P' tells us that it is a 'Power Car', meaning it contains the line collection equipment such as a pantograph. Some units will have differnt formations, especially when they have more cars.
Each unit will have its own number, that usually in some way fits into the larger MU number.With the 323s, the DMSO(A) on 323001 would be 64001, the PTSO 75001 (I believe 75) and the DMSO(B) 65001. On the 730/0s it would be (for 730001) 490001, 424001, and then 494001. What is interesting about this is that it steps up in increments of 2. On the 5-car 730/2s, it ascends perfectly, so for 730201, it would be 490201, 491201, 492201 etc. I found this interesting when I realised it. I will get onto the sub classes a little later.
